# Drevan writing guide

Drevan is a text-driven science-fantasy RPG. The people of Nexaris make a living among structures and devices they inherited without instructions. Magic and machinery belong in the same room. Preserve the tagline exactly.

> The old world is gone. The unknown awaits.

Consult [the lore reference](lore.md) before adding a claim about the world. Use [the roadmap](../planning/roadmap.md) for development plans.

## Write for the moment

| Content            | Approach                                                                                                           | Example                                                     |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Lore               | Attribute uncertain claims to a record, testimony, or belief. Leave a gap for the reader to consider.              | “Those who do speak of impossible architecture.”            |
| Location page      | Give the reader a concrete detail and orient them toward the activity.                                             | “Old healing apparatus hums in the quiet halls of Sanctum.” |
| Location directory | Use one short line that helps the player choose where to go.                                                       | “Pay gold to restore HP.”                                   |
| Item or creature   | Start with a distinct physical detail, ordinary use, or observed behavior. Keep effects and requirements explicit. | “A weathered blade with a sound edge beneath the rust.”     |
| System feedback    | Lead with the outcome or the action the player can take next. Keep repeated messages brief.                        | “You do not have enough gold to heal.”                      |

Give each location one or two details specific to its daily life. “A mysterious place full of ancient secrets” gives the player little to picture. The broken wiring a scavenger picks through tells the reader something about the animal and its surroundings.

## Humor

Keep the delivery matter-of-fact. Let the mismatch between an object’s reputation and its ordinary use deliver the joke. Use it sparingly, especially around serious lore and repeated gameplay feedback. Avoid memes, fourth-wall jokes, and consequences the game cannot deliver.

**Zhurak'Thul's Bog Wand** is a hypothetical example for tone.

> Zhurak'Thul spent forty years perfecting this instrument of unspeakable dark magic. It is primarily used to stir soup.

## Functional copy

Use [canonical terminology](terminology.md) for names and stats. Follow the [accessibility guide](../design/accessibility.md) for text alternatives and feedback. Captions can add setting or context.

- Use American English and sentence case for ordinary labels, buttons, and messages. Use “Log in” for the action. Save exclamation marks for exceptional moments.
- State units, costs, and reset times where players need them. Format dynamic numbers with the shared formatter and check zero, one, and several. Write “Restored 1 HP” and “Restored 50 HP” with the same unit.
- Use concise action labels and identify the destination in recovery links, as in “Heal at Sanctum.” Keep form instructions beside the inputs they govern.
